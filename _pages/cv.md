## Current Research Support

**Identifying Vocal Tract Gestures from Audio to Reimagine Biofeedback for Childhood Speech Sound Disorder**  
NIH NIDCD Pathway to Independence Award (K99/R00), 1K99DC023310-01A1  
Role: Principal Investigator. Five years of NIH funding across a mentored (K99) phase at the University of Maryland, College Park, and an independent (R00) phase. K99 phase: 07/01/2026 – 06/30/2028.  
This research aims to improve speech therapy for children with chronic speech sound disorder by creating an artificial intelligence pathway for automated, high-intensity vocal tract gesture biofeedback derived from audio alone. By engineering tools that estimate child vocal tract gestures from audio, classify vocal tract gesture errors to predict verbal feedback, and personalize tongue visual-biofeedback displays from vocal tract gestures, this project has the potential to expand use of best-practice therapy in the clinic, over telepractice, and at home. The research addresses a critical public health need to improve lifelong social, educational, and occupational outcomes for Americans with chronic speech sound disorders.
**Ultrasound Biofeedback Without the Ultrasound Machine: The Speech GLIMPSE Articulatory Display**  
American Speech-Language-Hearing Foundation Speech Science Research Grant. 12/01/2026 – 11/30/2027.  
Role: Principal Investigator  
This project deploys an AI articulatory biofeedback web app that enables researchers and clinicians to visualize the tongue without specialized equipment through acoustic-to-articulatory speech inversion neural networks. It also collects the first experimental evidence that viewing an articulatory display improves articulatory awareness.

**Interpretable and Available Acoustic-to-Articulatory Speech Inversion**  
American Speech-Language-Hearing Foundation New Investigators Research Grant. 12/01/2025 – 11/30/2026.  
Role: Principal Investigator  
This project makes speech inversion phonetically interpretable by normalizing speech inversion tract variables based on phonetic landmarks and improves speech inversion accuracy with multitask learning of phonetic data. Speech inversion will also be deployed as easy-to-use software, broadening access to kinematic speech science.

**Biofeedback-Enhanced Treatment for Speech Sound Disorder: Broadening Access through Telepractice and Artificial Intelligence**  
NIH 2R01DC017476. 01/01/2024 – 12/31/2028.  
Role: Co-Investigator. PI: T. McAllister.  
This research systematically evaluates the efficacy of visual-acoustic biofeedback intervention delivered in person versus via telepractice, and assesses whether maintenance of treatment gains can be enhanced through home practice with artificial intelligence (AI)-mediated feedback. It also tests whether efficacy can be optimized when the choice of biofeedback technology is personalized to the learner's profile of sensory strengths and limitations.

**Intensive Speech Motor Chaining Treatment and Artificial Intelligence Integration for Residual Speech Sound Disorders**  
NIH 1R01DC020959. 03/01/2023 – 02/29/2028.  
Role: Co-Investigator; co-designed and co-wrote the proposal. PI: J. Preston.  
This research addresses the need for sufficiently intense speech treatment by investigating intensive motor-based treatment bootcamps and AI-driven treatment. It tests an evidence-based service model in which a speech-language clinician and an AI clinician together overcome existing access barriers to intense treatment.


## Previous Research Support

**Quantifying Articulatory Performance in Children with Dysarthria: Development of an Automated Metric for Clinical Use**  
NIH 1R01DC019645. Role: Scientist, 01/2026 – 08/2026. PIs: K. Hustad and V. Berisha.  
This research refines and tests an automated tool for measuring speech sound development in children, identifying which speech sounds are not developing typically and which contribute most to speech intelligibility at different ages. The research benefits all children, with special application to children with cerebral palsy.

**Administrative Supplement to Support Collaborations to Improve the AI/ML-Readiness of NIH-Supported Data** (parent award: Biofeedback-Enhanced Treatment for Speech Sound Disorder: Randomized Controlled Trial and Delineation of Sensorimotor Subtypes)  
NIH 3R01DC017476-03S2. 08/15/2021 – 08/14/2022.  
Role: Named Senior/Key Personnel. PI: T. McAllister.  
The supplement laid the groundwork for automated speech recognition tools for speech sound disorder by augmenting an existing corpus of child speech recordings in preparation for sharing with AI/ML researchers.

**Innovative and Interdisciplinary Research Grant: Developing a Clinical Speech Recognition System for Childhood Speech Disorders**  
Syracuse University CUSE Grant II-14-2021. 06/01/2021 – 05/31/2023.  
Role: Named Key Personnel. PI: J. L. Preston.  
This project optimized a near real-time speech analysis algorithm (PERCEPT) to classify correct and incorrect /r/ sounds and integrated it into validated, computerized motor-based intervention software developed at Syracuse University (Speech Motor Chaining).
